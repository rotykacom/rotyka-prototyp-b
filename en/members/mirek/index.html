Mirek

  • Late Middle Ages
  • Thirty Years’ War
  • Equipment
  • Ancient Rome
  • Renaissance

He has been involved in martial arts since 1994. He began by training in various forms of self-defence, aikido and kendo. He has practised European armed martial techniques since 1996 and has been fully involved in historical fencing since 1997. He began in the LA-MA historical fencing group and studied fencing at Lars’s First Prague Fencing Hall. In 1999, he became a founding member of Rotyka. He later attended courses and classes at the Magistérium fencing school, initially under M. Khun and subsequently under B. “Borek” Belfín. Since 2018, he has also trained with the longsword as a member of the Guild of St Michael.

Besides fencing, he has practised historical archery since 1997, horse riding since 2005, occasionally including mounted fencing, and historical Ringen wrestling since 2008. He is a founding member of Ringen Club Prague. He speaks English.

His historical interests include the late Middle Ages, particularly the mid-15th century, the Thirty Years’ War in the first half of the 17th century, and ancient Rome in the 2nd century AD. He also has a secondary interest in other medieval and Renaissance periods.

History & heraldry

The Běškovec of Běškovice family

The minor nobleman Smil of Cítov is considered the ancestor and founder of the prominent Bohemian Běškovec of Běškovice family. He lived around the middle of the 13th century; a historical record from 1268 credits him with founding the monastery of St Lawrence near Mělník. The family’s arms featured a leaping unicorn, probably silver on a blue field, with golden horn and hooves. The family reached its greatest prominence between the 14th and 16th centuries, when bearers of these arms held important royal and archiepiscopal offices and acquired extensive property: further estates around Roudnice and Mělník, later also around Chrudim, as well as the stronghold of Jirny near Prague, among others. From the mid-14th century, the family was based at Běškovice, known since the 17th century as Horní Beřkovice in the Mělník district. An imposing château, the former family seat, still stands there. The family died out at the end of the 16th century.
