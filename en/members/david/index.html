People of Rotyka

David, known as “of Říčany”

  • Late Middle Ages
  • Thirty Years’ War

I have practised historical fencing since 1995. From around 1997, I was a member of the LA-MA historical fencing group, and in 1999 I became a founding member of Rotyka. I studied European fencing under Lars and, for a short time, at the Magisterium fencing school under Mirek Khun.
I focus on the second half of the 15th century and the Thirty Years’ War.

History & heraldry

The Lords of Říčany

An old Bohemian noble family named after the castle and town of Říčany. Its arms depict three silver water-lily leaves joined at the centre by their stems, forming a trefoil, on a red field.
Petr of Všechromy is regarded as the family’s ancestor; his sons were loyal supporters of King Přemysl Otakar II. Říčany Castle was founded around 1260, but it was only at the beginning of the 14th century that Oldřich, the chief provincial judge, used the designation “of Říčany”. Half a century later, the family had expanded considerably, owning Říčany, Průhonice and estates in many other places. Towards the end of that century, it finally divided into five main branches: Říčany; Ledeč; Hrádek and Kosova Hora; Malotice and Zásmuky; and Cehnice. The family also acquired Hořovice, Švihov, Štěkeň and Humpolec. Its members held office as district and provincial judges, governors—Adam was governor of both the Kouřim district and Prague’s Old Town—and imperial councillors. The family died out at the beginning of the 19th century.
