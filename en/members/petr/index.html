People of Rotyka

Petr

  • Late Middle Ages
  • Thirty Years’ War
  • Seven Years’ War

A bus stop called Na Návrší introduced me to this pastime. Thanks to it, I found my way, perhaps in 1995–1996, to training at Lars’s school of European martial arts. Around 1997, I also developed an appetite for “historical fencing” and joined the LA-MA group. From then on, everything happened very quickly.

I bought equipment, equipment and more equipment—all of which I later threw away after Rotyka was founded. Fortunately, Rotyka has a sound foundation, and after several years I still enjoy it and find it rewarding.

I try to devote myself as thoroughly as possible to the period after the Hussite Wars and to the Thirty Years’ War, with a more casual interest in the Anglo-French wars in 18th-century Canada.

History & heraldry

The Leskovec of Leskovec family

The coat of arms I use at battles originally belonged to the Leskovec of Leskovec family. This name belonged to an old Bohemian family of minor nobles who were later elevated to the higher nobility. They came from the stronghold and village of Leskovec near Pelhřimov. The family’s founder, Jan, lived at the beginning of the 15th century; a surviving record dates from 1424, when he died in the Hussite Wars. The family died out in 1680 with the death of Jan Kryštof of Leskovec. Its arms showed two crossed silver pitchforks on a red field.
I do not portray this particular family directly. For my own purposes, I changed the pitchforks on the arms to gold and placed the fictional family in Jíloviště near Prague. These days, I tend instead to portray a hired fighting man and enter the service of banner-bearing nobles.
