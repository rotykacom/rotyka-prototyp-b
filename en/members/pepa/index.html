History & heraldry

The Lords of Víckov

The Lords of Víckov are mentioned in reliable sources from the beginning of the 14th century and rank among the important Moravian families. More detailed information dates from 1353, when Bohuslav of Víckov held the office of provincial judge in Moravia. Somewhat later, the family gave up its ancestral castle, Víckov near Tišnov, which was already in ruins by the end of the 15th century, and moved to a new seat at Prusinovice. They nevertheless retained their territorial designation after Víckov Castle, and in the 16th century began calling themselves the Prusinovský of Víckov family. Their arms were a shield divided vertically: red on the heraldic right, and two black bars on a white field on the left.
Two contrasting figures are among the family’s most famous members: Vilém Prusinovský, Bishop of Olomouc, who died in 1572 and was renowned for his vigorous Counter-Reformation policy; and Jan Adam of Víckov, who took part in the Bohemian Revolt of 1618–1620 and went into exile after the Battle of White Mountain. Among the exiles, he was one of the most ardent advocates of uncompromising resistance to the Habsburgs and led by example with weapons in hand. In 1623, he instigated and led a major Wallachian uprising in eastern Moravia and distinguished himself as a brave commander in several victorious battles.
The Lords of Víckov died out in the 17th century.
